## What the four rows above attribute

no limiter                   7396 req/s
bounded keys, slow refill    6871 req/s   (-7.1% vs no limiter)
unbounded keys, fast refill  6786 req/s   (-1.2% vs bounded)
unbounded keys, slow refill  1201 req/s   (-82.3% vs fast refill)

Two variables, four runs, one moving at a time. Read down the second
column:

* the limiter on a bounded key space is free;
* an unbounded key space is also nearly free -- so long as the sweep
can drop what it looks at;
* the cost is entirely in the last step, where a slow refill means
the sweep examines every entry and is entitled to evict none, then
does it again on the next request over a table that grew.

So the hazard is not "many keys". It is many keys *and* a quota
whose refill is slower than the run, which is the shape of every
per-hour login or password-reset throttle. A per-minute or
per-second quota over the same key space does not pay it.

All four runs are 20s at 128 connections against the same route.
